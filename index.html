Game assets

Replace the game's inventory icons, textures, sounds and music with your own files. Open a heading below. Everything is kept in this browser on this computer: it is not part of a save game and other co-op players do not see or hear it.

Name each PNG after its item ID (e.g. smoked_ham.png) and import as many as you like. Transparent PNGs work and any size is scaled to the item's grid footprint. Limits: PNG only, 6 MB a file, 4096 × 4096 px.

Name each image after its texture ID (e.g. brick.png, zombie_skin.png). Export the defaults to get every name and a picture to paint over. Changes show at once. Limits: PNG, JPG or WebP; 16 MB a file; 4096 × 4096 px. World surfaces are stored at 512 × 512.

All texture rules and limits
  • World surfaces (walls, roads, roofs, ground: 47 of them) are stored at 512 × 512. Any other size is resized, and a non-square image is stretched. They repeat across walls and ground, so use an image that tiles.
  • The game tints each surface towards the colour of the building it is on. Detail and contrast carry over fully; a strong colour of your own is pulled towards the building's.
  • Normal maps use the ID with _n (e.g. brick_n.png). Only red and green are read; match the exported default's green direction. Roughness and ambient occlusion cannot be replaced.
  • Character textures (zombie, survivor) are atlases: paint over the exported default and keep every part where it is. Transparency is ignored.
  • JPG and WebP are lossy: use PNG for normal maps. Large character textures use more video memory (a 4096 px texture takes about 90 MB).
  • Not replaceable here: props, furniture, vehicles, weapons, the sky and the interface.
  • Clearing this browser's site data removes custom textures.

Name each file after its sound ID (e.g. groan.wav, shot_shotgun.mp3, music_game.mp3). Export the defaults to get every name. Limits: MP3 or WAV; sound effects 8 MB and 30 seconds a file; music 80 MB a track.

All sound rules and limits
  • MP3: MPEG-1/2 Layer III, constant or variable bit rate, 32 to 320 kbps. 128 to 192 kbps is plenty for effects.
  • WAV: uncompressed PCM at 8, 16 or 24 bit, or 32 bit float. Compressed WAV (ADPCM, µ-law) may not load.
  • Mono or stereo, any sample rate. Everything is converted to your sound card's rate (usually 48 kHz), so files above 48 kHz gain nothing.
  • Sound effects are held uncompressed in memory once loaded: about 11 MB for every minute of stereo. Hence the 30 second limit.
  • Variants: add _2 to _9 to the name (groan.wav, groan_2.wav) and the game picks one at random each time. Up to 9 for each sound; music takes one file.
  • Music is streamed and loops. 80 MB holds about 7.5 minutes of 16 bit 44.1 kHz stereo WAV, or over 30 minutes of 320 kbps MP3. An MP3 loop has a short gap where it restarts; a WAV loops cleanly.
  • MP3 files begin with a few hundredths of a second of silence. Use WAV for sharp sounds such as gunshots.
  • Zombies, shots, doors and glass are placed in the world: the game fades them with distance and pans them left and right. Mono, dry (no echo) and loud works best.
  • The game does not level volumes. Match your files to the exported defaults. The volume sliders in Options › Sound still apply.
  • The default sounds are made by code, so "Export default sounds" records them to WAV; each export differs slightly. In-game and siege music have no file: they are exported as a recording of the generated notes.
  • Not replaceable here: reload stages of the detailed gun handling, wind and rain, and the water splashes.
  • Total space is your browser's storage allowance (normally several hundred MB or more). Clearing this browser's site data removes custom sounds.
